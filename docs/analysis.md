# Downstream analyses and result figures

Run the analysis after training and comparator evaluation have produced the score
tables. All generated files are written under the supplied work directory.

```sh
python scripts/analyze.py --work-dir runs/example \
  --cognition data/raw/adni_cognition.csv --seed 42
python scripts/figures.py --work-dir runs/example
```

Both commands accept `--rscript /path/to/Rscript`. Python uses the installed
package's R resources; `scripts/r` also provides source-checkout entry points.
Figure 1 is a supplied model diagram and is not generated by this workflow.

## Input tables

`data/evaluation/train_scores.csv` and `test_scores.csv` require:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `RID` | Participant identifier |
| `PHC_Age_PET` | PET acquisition age in years |
| `PL_DAPS` | Uncalibrated posterior mean for PACE-Aβ |
| `Centiloids` | Global amyloid burden |
| `SLOPE` | SLOPE score, oriented toward increasing stage |
| `dEBM_upstream` | Continuous dEBM event score, oriented toward increasing stage |

The `PL_DAPS` key is retained as the exchange-table identifier. Figures display
the method as PACE-Aβ. All four score columns must be finite. Participant/age
keys must be unique, and train/test participants must be disjoint.

`data/prepared/train_input.csv` and `test_input.csv` supply the same visit keys,
`PHC_Diagnosis` (`1`=CN, `2`=MCI, `3`=AD), `PHC_Sex` (`1`=male, `2`=female),
and `PHC_Education` (years). Preparation excludes participants whose first
otherwise eligible PET visit has missing or other diagnosis before splitting
or cross-validation. Among retained participants, later visits with missing or
other diagnosis are excluded from the diagnosis panel and from baseline CN/MCI
eligibility, but remain in temporal evaluation.

The cognition CSV requires `RID`, `PHC_Age_Cognition` (years), `PHC_MEM`,
`PHC_MEM_SE`, `PHC_Diagnosis`, `PHC_Sex`, and `PHC_Education`. It must contain
harmonized visit-specific diagnosis and participant sex/education. The workflow
does not infer nearest diagnoses or merge external demographic files. Sex is
encoded as `1`/`M`/`male` for male and `2`/`F`/`female` for female. Cognitive
rows need unique participant/age keys; rows with unavailable scores, ages, or
nonpositive/missing measurement SE are ineligible.

## Statistical definitions

The demographic table counts participants once, using their first eligible PET
row after cohort selection. Diagnosis, PET age, sex, and education are all taken
from that same row, before covariate imputation. Later visits do not fill missing
baseline values or change the participant's diagnostic group. The table describes
all analyzed participants together in CN, MCI, and AD columns; it does not display
the training/test split. Machine-readable audit summaries retain overall totals
and also provide each split separately. Age and education use observed baseline values and
sample SD (`ddof=1`); available counts and missing values are recorded. A mean or
SD requiring unavailable observations is reported as null (SD needs at least two
observations). Invalid sex codes, infinite covariates, duplicate PET keys, and
overlapping training/test participants are rejected.

Each method receives a separate training-only empirical midrank map. Every
participant has total weight one, divided equally across visits. Training score
endpoints map to zero and one. Test scores use the same linear interpolation
and strictly increasing tail extrapolation based on 5% of the training support;
they are not clipped or recalibrated using testing data.

Temporal evaluation uses consecutive visits sorted by PET acquisition age. A
violation is a strict score decrease; ties do not count. The violation ratio
divides violations by all adjacent pairs. The conditional violation gap is the
mean positive decline among violations. At thresholds 0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.075,
and 0.1, qualifying declines are strictly larger than the threshold; the full
decline is retained, not its excess over the threshold. Undefined conditional
means when there are no violations are recorded as null.

Fig.2A and Fig.2C use the same two-sided subject-cluster bootstrap-t test of
pooled mean differences. Fig.2A compares CN with MCI and MCI with AD. A
participant contributes the sum and count of visit scores in each diagnosis
group; a participant can contribute to more than one group. Fig.2C compares
PACE with SLOPE, Centiloids, and dEBM. Each participant contributes a sum of
positive declines and a count of violations for each method. CVG is the ratio
of these totals across participants, rather than an average of
participant-specific CVGs. Strict ties contribute neither positive gaps nor
violations.

Each resample draws participants with replacement and keeps all their eligible
visits or adjacent pairs together, including every diagnosis group or method.
Participants absent from a diagnosis group or without violations contribute
zero to its sum and count and remain in the resampling population. This retains
within-participant and cross-group or cross-method dependence.

Both panels use 100,000 resamples, seed 42, and batches of 512. A participant
influence-function sandwich provides the standard error of each mean contrast
in the observed sample and every resample. The bootstrap statistic centers
each resampled contrast at the observed contrast before studentization. The
two-sided p-value is `(1 + exceedances) / (B + 1)`, using absolute t statistics.
The symmetric 95% bootstrap-t interval uses the 95th percentile of the absolute
centered bootstrap statistics. Undefined observed contrasts or resamples stop
inference; they are never removed or redrawn. The procedure assumes independent
participants and positive population probabilities for the relevant diagnosis
groups or violations, and conditions on the fitted models and training rank
maps. It does not repeat model fitting or rank calibration.

P-values are reported without multiple-comparison adjustment. All bootstrap
p-values below 0.0001 are displayed as `p < 0.0001` in both panels, regardless
of whether a resample exceeds the observed statistic; other p-values use four
decimal places. The 0.0001 threshold is a common reporting rule, not the
minimum possible plus-one estimate. Raw estimates, exceedance counts,
Monte Carlo uncertainty, effect estimates, standard errors, and confidence
intervals remain in `reports/analysis/diagnosis_tests.json` and
`reports/analysis/conditional_gap_tests.json`. These tests concern pooled mean
differences, not equality of the full distributions or their medians.
Both boxplots in Fig.2A/C use transparent outlier markers without excluding
observations from their statistics or tests. The gap plot's display range
is based on the visible whiskers and comparison brackets. Its p-value labels
are centered above their respective brackets and use 6.5 pt at the native
manuscript figure width.

The continuous piecewise model uses an intercept, score, and positive hinge.
The breakpoint minimizes training OLS SSE over the central 80% of training
scores, using a 2,001-point profile and local bounded refinement. This single
training knot is fixed for test curves and cognitive grouping. Segment
significance is never a selection criterion. Segment OLS annotations are descriptive visit-level analyses.

## Memory analysis

The first valid PET defines each test participant's imaging baseline. Baseline
CN/MCI participants with Centiloids ≤30 enter the default analysis; change this
explicitly with `--centiloid-max`. Early/late groups are defined by the fixed
training breakpoint. The cognitive anchor is the closest valid measurement to
baseline PET, with earlier cognition winning distance ties. All valid cognition
at or after that anchor is retained. Follow-up time is age minus anchor age.
The anchor may precede PET; no additional time window or minimum follow-up count
is imposed.

Baseline Centiloids are matched using pooled type-8 quantile deciles. Each bin
retains the smaller group count, sampled without replacement with seed 42.
The outcome model is:

```text
PHC_MEM ~ time_year * group + baseline_age_centered + sex + education_centered
          + time_year:baseline_age_centered
variance = diag(PHC_MEM_SE²) + participant random-intercept variance
           + independent observation residual variance
```

Models use `metafor::rma.mv`, REML estimation, and two-sided Wald z tests with
normal 95% confidence intervals. Age is cognition-anchor age; age and education
are centered over analyzable pre-match observation rows. Age enters as a main
effect and interacts with time; there are no random time slopes. Group slopes
and plotted trajectories use the common reference age (`baseline_age_centered=0`).
Without a group-by-age-by-time interaction, the group slope difference is the
same at every common age. Diagnosis is an eligibility variable, not an additional
covariate. Matched Centiloids do not imply balance in age or diagnosis. Inference
is conditional on the trained scores, breakpoint, and realized matching.

## Outputs and figures

`data/analysis` contains all four rank-calibrated tables, maps, adjacent-pair
declines, threshold metrics, diagnosis observations, the training knot profile,
fixed-knot fits, and the memory input PET table. Its `memory` directory contains
selected/matched samples, models, coefficients, covariance matrices, slope
contrasts, and prediction grids. `reports/analysis/summary.json` collects the
manuscript numerical results; logs and geometry records are stored alongside it.
`reports/analysis/demographics.json` and `demographics.csv` contain aggregate
participant demographics by split and baseline diagnosis. The generated
`table1_demographics.tex` contains a compact three-rule table and its notes;
include it inside a LaTeX table float without additional packages. For an 86-mm
column, use 9-pt table text with 10.45-pt leading, 0.8-pt column padding (`tabcolsep`), and
`arraystretch=1.1`. These outputs are also regenerated by the analysis stage and contain no participant identifiers.

Figures 2–3 preserve the plotting primitives, palettes, annotation rules, and
fixed physical coordinate rectangles used by the manuscript. Fig.2 includes
the diagnosis boxplot, zero-threshold violation-ratio bars, and per-violation
gap boxplots. Fig.3 combines the training/test piecewise fits (A/B), matched
Centiloid distributions (C), and memory trajectories (D) in a two-by-two layout.
Both piecewise panels use the same training knot. Panel D uses the
saved model, with fixed-effect mean trajectories at centered age/education and
female sex; bands are pointwise mean confidence intervals, not individual
prediction intervals. The shared time grid follows the original plotting rule.
All panels and composites are generated from numerical data and code; no
external picture assets are read. Composite assembly preserves vector scale.
All four Fig.3 coordinate rectangles are 65 by 50 mm on 81 by 75 mm panels.
C/D have single-row legends below their x-axis labels. Assembly removes a
verified blank 6 mm band below A/B and uses a 1 mm row gap, giving a 162 by
145 mm composite. The shared layout configuration also
places every Fig.2/3 panel letter 0.8 mm from its panel's left edge, with its
baseline 3.7 mm below the top. Label fonts and placement use the same Python
renderer for both Python and R panels. Fig.2 has a native width of 178 mm.
At the manuscript's 86 mm width, Fig.3's four plotting areas each measure
34.51 by 26.54 mm. Fig.3 uses 10 pt ticks and legend text, 11 pt axis and
legend titles, 12 pt plot titles, 9.5 pt statistical annotations, and 11 pt
panel letters in the standalone panels; manuscript scaling applies uniformly
to these sizes. Fig.2 panel letters remain 10 pt.

Matplotlib prefers Arial and falls back to DejaVu Sans when unavailable. R uses
the system sans-serif family. Numerical reproduction is independent of fonts;
pixel comparisons require the same fonts and rendering stack. The validated
R stack is R 4.5.1, dplyr 1.2.1, tidyr 1.3.2, metafor 5.0-1, Matrix 1.7-5,
ggplot2 4.0.3, gtable 0.3.6, and jsonlite 2.0.0. Python dependencies are pinned
in the project configuration.
